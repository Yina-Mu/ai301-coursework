# Voice guide: how I talk upstream

## Who I am in threads

I'm a CS grad student working through a course assignment in this repo. I'm here to reproduce the reported issue and post evidence of what I find — not to fix it. Expect plain comments: what I ran, what I saw, and what I'll do next.

## Rules I write by

### Rule: sound-like-a-person

Write like a person typing in a thread, not a press release. No hype, no filler openers, no flattery.

- Wrong: "I'm absolutely thrilled to take on this fascinating issue — amazing catch, by the way!"
- Right: "I'm picking up this issue for a course assignment. I'll reproduce it and post what I find."

### Rule: plain-english

Short sentences, ordinary words. If a sentence needs three commas to survive, split it.

- Wrong: "Due to the fact that the parser is currently unable to handle the aforementioned input, a crash is resultant."
- Right: "The parser crashes because it calls .items() on a list."

### Rule: name-the-specifics

Every comment names the exact version or commit, the exact behavior, and the concrete next step. Nothing that could be pasted onto another issue.

- Wrong: "I will investigate this issue and post an update soon."
- Right: "I'll reproduce this on commit 1a2b3c4 and post the terminal output here."

### Rule: promise-only-the-investigation

Promise the report, never the fix, never a date.

- Wrong: "I'll have a fix ready by Friday."
- Right: "I'll post my reproduction notes here once I've run them."

## Things I never post

- A promised fix or a promised date.
- Flattery, hype, or filler openers ("Great issue!", "Thrilled to dive in").
- Vague status with no evidence ("Looking into it, will update soon").
- A claim about behavior I have not run myself.
- Apologies or hedging that pad the comment instead of adding information.
